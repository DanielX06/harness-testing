WHAT IS KNOWN ABOUT THE DEVICE AND THE GRID (operator documentation)
- The device is an inverter-based unit rated 900 MVA. Its terminal is bus 3, which connects to bus 9 through its step-up transformer (see system_diagram.png).
- Its documentation says it supports grid frequency through active-power droop and regulates voltage through reactive-power droop. Its gains and time constants are not documented. Identifying its terminal behaviour is the task.
- The operator sets its active-power dispatch and its voltage schedule. runs.json gives both for each recording, and voltage_setpoint_pu logs the setpoint over time.
- The rest of the network is a ten-bus, two-area system with three synchronous generators, each with a governor and an exciter. Loads are constant impedance, so their P and Q scale with V squared. No secondary control restores frequency, so after an event the frequency can settle away from nominal and the bus angle keeps drifting.
- Short-circuit study at the device terminal: at short time scales (tens of milliseconds) the rest of the network, seen from bus 3, behaves like a voltage source behind about 0.5 pu reactance on the device's 900 MVA rating (short-circuit ratio about 2). Slower behaviour (the generators' controls, machines swinging against each other) is not part of that equivalent. tools/grid_equivalent_replay.py uses it.
- The manufacturer documents the control structure, though not its gains and time constants. tools/structure_menu.py builds it (run python tools/structure_menu.py list): an internal angle driven by frequency droop with a PI power loop, a reactive droop with a PI reactive loop feeding the voltage reference, a PI voltage loop in the internal frame and a PI current loop behind a filter reactance. tools/fit_structure.py fits its parameters to the recordings.
